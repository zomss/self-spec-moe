# c3 — deciding online what cannot be predicted offline

Contribution 3 of 4. **Status: DRAFT (2026-08-06)** — measurement arc
complete (phase 96, W0–W9b); two claims retracted during the arc and
recorded as such; writing in progress. Values in `paper/data/c3_*.json`
(to be extracted); full protocol, pre-registrations and gates:
`research/96_selector_foundations/`.

## design

**Claim.** The two quantities that set speculative throughput have
different epistemic status, and a deployable selector must be built
around that asymmetry: cost (R) is identifiable offline, acceptance (f)
is not. A selector that respects the split arms where speculation pays,
recovers the loss an offline-only selector concedes, and — where
acceptance cannot be predicted at all — fails closed rather than
guessing. All four architectures in this record are decided by that
one rule, with three different outcomes.

**Falsifiable form.** If offline selection were within noise of a
selector with live feedback, C3 would be unnecessary: ship C2's table
and stop. The measured *error floor* is the falsification target, and
it is not noise (below). Conversely, if live feedback could rescue any
architecture, the fail-closed branch would be a bug rather than a
result; llama shows it is not.

**The dichotomy (the structural claim).** From C2's identity
S = (1+fK)/(KR+1):

| quantity | depends on | offline identifiability | consequence for the selector |
|---|---|---|---|
| R (cost) | cell (batch × ctx), architecture, realization | **yes** — reproduced at 1.00× under an audited protocol (W4b) | Round 1: sound cost-only elimination |
| f (acceptance) | regime × *content* | **no** — ρ ∈ [0.41, 1.23] across content draws; llama separability failure 35% | Round 2: measure; never predict |

The identity's "+1" is itself architecture-dependent and was corrected
during this arc: for sparse-activation models, K+1 verify tokens
activate ~min(E, (K+1)k) experts, so verify is *not* one target step
below expert-coverage saturation. The deployed form is

    S = (1 + fK) / (K·D/T + V(K,b) + C/T)

with D the draft step, V the verify cost in target-step units (=1 dense,
coverage-scaled for MoE), C the per-armed-step serving-stack overhead.

**The error floor (why online feedback is necessary).** Freezing the
offline argmax and scoring it against the live best, per regime:

| arch | mean loss | worst cell |
|---|---|---|
| dense | **+1.75%** | +3.6% (R5) |
| llama | **+7.05%** | **+37.1% (R1)** |

Dense sits inside the predicted 1–3% band by the predicted mechanism
(arming where live says OFF; window mis-picks). Llama exceeds it: the
offline pick's *deployed realization* collapses (w512 predicted 1.168,
realized 0.745, against w2048's 1.116). This is the quantity C3 must
recover, and it is why the contribution is a selector rather than a
table.

**The design, in three stages with per-stage guarantees.**

1. *Round 1 — sound cost-only elimination.* Eliminate (comp, K) at a
   cell iff sup_f S < 1 + ε, using the favourable end of the measured
   R band. **Guarantee:** S is monotone in f, so the bound holds over
   ALL acceptance behaviours — Round 1 never removes the true optimum.
   Completeness is empirical. Within the skip lever it decides only the
   COUNT ladder, never the identity: count → R is physics, identity →
   f is content-dependent and Round-2-only.
2. *Round 2 — real-acceptance calibration on the survivors.* Measure f
   per (regime, composition) — not per cell (f is cell-independent) —
   with unconditional bursts, per-K counters, natural EOS, ≥2 content
   seeds. **Guarantee:** f̂ concentrates as a Bernoulli rate; the
   surviving intervals shrink below the deciding margins. Only Round 2
   may ARM.
3. *Serving — ranked ladder with sequential demotion.* Walk the
   Round-2 ranking, arm the first option clearing break-even on
   measured τ, demote on live evidence, probe for re-promotion.
   **Guarantee:** regret is bounded by the ranking's tail, and the
   arming test uses the same identity as Round 1, so a mis-ranked
   option cannot be armed below break-even.

The asymmetry is the doctrine: **Round 1 can only prove OFF, never
ARM.** Arming always waits for measured acceptance. Fail-closed is
therefore the default, not a fallback.

## evaluation

Four architectures (dense Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B, MLA DeepSeek-V2-Lite,
MoE Qwen3-30B-A3B) on H100 80GB, one serving stack. Every scored run:
`notune` (flashinfer autotune off — a measured 40% boot lottery, W1),
episode rejection against a 2nd-highest-round anchor, cross-boot
certification, and a binding pre-registration committed before data
exists (W3). Decision-critical b1 cells prefer the GPU1 lane: every
suppressed cell in this arc sat on GPU0 (co-tenant episode dossier).

**Currency.** Two are reported and never mixed: S_e2e (wall-clock, the
deployment currency) and S_dec (per-request decode rate, the analogue
of C2's compile-cell currency). Their gap is real (up to +4.4% on
dense R5) and mixing them produced one retracted claim in an earlier
phase.

**Equal work (W9, added mid-arc).** Cost-side verdicts near S ≈ 1
require a constant-batch-width protocol (`G93_FIXED_LEN`). Under
natural EOS with heavy-tailed regimes, a fixed prompt batch drains and
the two arms *generate different text* — at T=0 speculation is
distribution-preserving in theory, but verify runs the target at
different batch shapes, FP reduction order changes, near-tie argmaxes
diverge and EOS lands elsewhere. Measured: out p95 1332 (AR) vs 925
(spec). The arm with the shorter tail leaves the inefficient low-batch
phase sooner and posts a higher rate with **no per-step speedup**.
This invalidated one verdict in this document (below) and is the
protocol rule for all marginal cost-side cells.

### dense — the gate passes on measurement

Unconditional K4, pool {skip 2,8 | none} × {w512 | w2048}, seeds 0+1
(disjoint content), S_dec:

| comp | R5 | R5cot | R1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| s-2,8 / w512 | 1.278 | **1.884** | 1.161 |
| s-2,8 / w2048 | 1.442 | 1.873 | **1.184** |
| s-none / w512 | **1.532** | 1.857 | 1.125 |
| s-none / w2048 | 1.454 | 1.827 | 1.148 |

Best static is s-none/w512 (3-regime mean 1.505). Per-regime selection
beats it by +2.8% (R5cot) and **+5.9%** (R1) — clearing the
pre-registered single-regime branch (≥5% at mean ≥ 0). Acceptance
agrees across disjoint content seeds to 0.5–1.5% (worst 6%), so the
intervals are seed-tight and every regime is rankable.

**The skip axis is fractal.** A full 36-layer sensitivity profile
(36/36 boots) shows the cheap tier is exclusively early-mid layers and
depth-monotone at full resolution. Extending the count ladder:

| count | R5 f / e2e | R1 f / e2e |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.857 / 523 | 0.952 / 169 |
| 2 {2,8} | 0.813 / 450 | 0.935 / 174 |
| 4 {2,7,8,11} | 0.740 / 499 | 0.924 / 167 |
| 8 {2,3,4,7,8,11,15,16} | 0.612 / 484 | 0.841 / **192** |

Count-8 is the new R1 winner (e2e 192 ≈ S 1.25, **+9%** over the
previous best), and deep skipping pays at b1 math while losing at b8
RAG — the count axis is regime-dependent exactly as window is. With
count in the pool the gate's R1 margin widens from +5.9% to **~+15%**.

### the runtime: two fixes and a ladder

The deployed selector was **two compensating errors** (W2): a biased
acceptance EMA and an argmax over predicted rather than measured
throughput. Both are fixed:

- *EMA bias (F5).* Optimistic-1.0 pooling was re-injected into the
  running average, inflating it by +0.1 (0.647 vs true 0.531).
  Optimism is now cold-start only.
- *Position-resolved counters.* Chain-censored per-position evidence
  yields τ(K) = 1 + Σp_i unbiased for **every** K from one stream —
  one armed configuration now calibrates the whole ladder.
- *Ranked ladder.* Smoke: 190.5 tok/s = 97% of the unconditional
  ceiling, against ~152 for the argmax it replaces — a **+25%**
  recovery. Validation: R5 ~465 > argmax ~452; b1 R1 [178–181] above
  the unconditional ceiling 168 with 1858/1858 steps at K=4 (zero
  flapping).

### llama — the fail-closed branch is a result

Llama's physics permit a win (R < 1 exists). What fails is prediction:
acceptance measured on calibration content does not transfer to
deployment content (35% separability failure; ρ as low as 0.41). The
offline argmax is therefore unpredictable — the +37.1% worst-cell floor
above is llama's. The selector detects non-transferability and ships a
static piecewise configuration with the gate OFF. **A selector that
cannot rank must not arm**; this is the doctrine's load-bearing case,
not an omission.

### MLA / MoE — where the gate is the whole answer

Below b32 both architectures are OFF for a reason no acceptance can
overturn: break-even τ* = 4R+1 exceeds the maximum possible τ = 5 at
K=4. Demonstrated live — MoE R6 b8 runs at τ = 4.81 (f = 0.95) and
still loses 19%. Certified cells span S = 0.49–0.96 with back-out
R̂ = 1.06–2.25.

**MLA b32 arms** at S = 1.111–1.129 across all three regimes, and the
arm is protocol-robust: 1.064 under constant-width measurement, 1.129
under natural EOS. (Content flag: V2-Lite loops at T=0, so its τ ≈ 5.0
is loop-inflated; the OFF verdicts are a fortiori sound.)

**MoE arms nowhere in the cells W7 examined** (R1/R2/R6 at b1/b8/b32)
— see the retraction below. An equal-work sweep of the full 33-cell
Stage-B surface (W10) finds 5 marginal wins, all at b≥32, none above
**+3.6%**, and only 3 clearing a 1.5% arming rent. The precise claim is
therefore "no MoE cell wins by a margin that pays for the switching
machinery", not "no MoE cell beats AR". The same sweep corrects c1's
MoE row from 8/33 to 5/33 and identifies a second, architecture-general
inflation: Stage-B AR anchors ran 3–14% slow under autotune, which
inflates every S uniformly (R6 b1 is a drain-immune control at +9.7%
with identical output length).

### why R > 1: the mechanism is dispatch, not bandwidth

Direct step-level profiling (W8), constant-width protocol, T from
unperturbed AR arms:

| | MLA b1 → b32 | MoE b1 → b32 |
|---|---|---|
| draft step vs its own byte roofline | **7.8× → 2.2×** | **15.2× → 2.1×** |
| verify excess V | 1.75 → 1.19 | 1.54 → 1.34 |
| stack overhead per armed step | 0.79 T → 0.42 T | 0.83 T → 0.49 T |

The draft step is dispatch-bound, not bandwidth-bound: quantization
shrinks bytes, but a launch-bound step does not get faster when its
bytes shrink. MoE's gap is double MLA's because 128 experts × 768
intermediate make per-expert GEMMs at M = 1 nearly pure launch
overhead. Verify excess is second and decays toward saturation exactly
as the coverage law predicts — the i.i.d. bytes law *over*-predicts V
below saturation (2.37 vs 1.75) and converges at b32, because below
saturation the step is latency-bound and extra verify tokens do not
cost their bytes.

**Theory alone cannot eliminate a single cell.** The HBM roofline
floor gives S_max = 2.07–3.49 > 1 in all six profiled cells. Offline
profiling is therefore a *necessary* Round-1 input, not a refinement —
the roofline-to-achievable gap is 2–15× and no model closes it.

### measurement methodology (a contribution in its own right)

Three protocol results this arc, each forced by a wrong number:

1. *Autotune is a lottery* (W1): 40% boot-to-boot variance until
   disabled; every scored run in the arc is `notune`.
2. *Realization × decode length* (W4c): the scratchpad realization
   scores S = 0.846 at ~72-token decodes vs the piecewise 1.420, and
   the two are equivalent (<2%) at 512-token decodes. Realization is
   not a free axis.
3. *Repeatable ≠ comparable* (W9): the natural-EOS b32 measurement is
   stable to 0.9% across 8 rounds and still produced a wrong ARM
   verdict, because the arms did different amounts of work. Only a
   workload-controlled re-measurement caught it.

A sweep of 726 Stage-B cells for the same artifact: 19% contaminated,
6% (43 cells) contaminated *and* near S = 1 (flippable). Two
mitigations: winner-map picks are spec-vs-spec so the shared AR anchor
cancels exactly (only ARM/OFF gate decisions carry the artifact in
full); and MLA is 0% contaminated *by construction* (its ceiling
protocol clips all arms to equal lengths) — which is why its b32 arm
reproduced across protocols and MoE's did not.

### status: DRAFT (2026-08-06)

**Retractions (T11 — retained with their disproofs):**

- *MoE b32/R2 arms (P-W7d "refuted").* Reported at S = 1.087 certified
  on both boots. **Withdrawn.** Under equal-work measurement the cell
  loses at both engine configurations (0.928, 0.943) with acceptance
  unchanged (τ 4.45 vs 4.43 — never an acceptance effect). The C2
  oracle's OFF prediction was correct. The paper consequently loses its
  "the two-round design recovers a win the oracle missed" exhibit; the
  replacement claim is the methodological one above.
- *P-W8c "validated" (cost-model reconstruction).* The
  coverage-corrected reconstruction was an algebraic identity
  (recon/meas ≡ coverage) that holds for any cost model, right or
  wrong. **Withdrawn**; P-W8c remains untested. A real test needs
  out-of-sample prediction (e.g. predict K=2 or b64 from terms measured
  elsewhere).

**Permanent disclosures:**

- The profiling instrument perturbs armed-step cost by **25–29%**
  (measured, W8d). Stage-0's OFF proofs therefore survive only at b1;
  b8/b32 verdicts rest on unperturbed serving runs, not on the cost
  model. Certifying Stage 0 needs a CUDA-event/nsys profiler.
- MLA b32's ARM carries a content flag (loop-inflated τ) and needs
  real-content confirmation before deployment.
- 43 Stage-B cells are near-1 and drain-contaminated; none carries a
  headline. Any cell this paper claims must be re-measured under
  `G93_FIXED_LEN` or stated as unresolved.
- The co-tenant episode dossier: every suppressed cell in this arc sat
  on the GPU0 lane; the lane effect is an offset (it ran HIGH in the
  W7 tie-breaker), not a one-sided suppression.

**Open (not blocking the claim):** A100 column (hardware access);
unperturbed profiler; re-measurement of any claimed suspect cell.
