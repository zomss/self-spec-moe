## design

**Claim.** Given a new (model, hardware, regime), a bounded measurement
budget finds a configuration within a few percent of the exhaustive
optimum over the COMPOSED lever space — and no proxy-scoring shortcut
can replace those measurements.

The negative half was settled earlier (phase 90): five proxy classes
falsified, so acceptance cannot be predicted, only measured. C2
supplies the positive half — a search with a MEASURED regret-vs-budget
curve against an exhaustive oracle.

**Why composition is the body.** Single levers (~13/arch) are
brute-forceable; C1 did exactly that. Composed, the space is
5 quant x 5 window x 3 skip x 2 kvq x 4 K x ~2 kernels ~ 1400 configs
per cell, ~30k per architecture column. Exhaustive is impossible, so
search becomes the contribution rather than a convenience. (What
counts as a lever — training-free, distribution-preserving,
self-draft, one serving stack — is defined by the inclusion criterion
in c1.md; the O(#levers + #confirmations) cost below is also the
price of ADMITTING a new lever class: one profile, not a redesign.)

**The factorization (the key structural claim).** The three quantities
behave differently and are separated accordingly:

| stage | quantity | depends on | how obtained |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | cost R | cell (batch x ctx) — measured CV 14.5% across cells, so NOT profilable | measure for singles per cell; predict compositions additively |
| B | acceptance p_i per depth | regime only — measured CV 2.2-3.3% across cells | measurement-only (phase-90 theorem); predict compositions from singles; confirm finalists |
| C | K and the final pick | neither — derived | argmax_K tau(K)/(K R(K)+1) in closed form |

K separates cleanly because with a SCALAR f, dS/dK = (f-R)/(KR+1)^2 is
sign-constant — K would always be K_max or OFF, contradicting every
measured cell. Acceptance decays with depth (measured -1.8% for
win8192 to -40.9% for win128 from K2->K6), and that decay is what
creates the interior optimum. One profile at K_max reconstructs tau(K)
for all smaller K (verified: 0.00% reconstruction error).

**The formal skeleton — what is derived, what is measured, what is
bounded.** The search is not a heuristic pipeline; each stage has a
stated basis, and every assumption carries an error bar from our own
controls:

1. *Measurement is NECESSARY (impossibility).* Phase 90 falsified five
   proxy classes for acceptance (angular importance inverted at -0.77;
   micro-LOO 0.34; CLaSp-cosine 0.37; learned predictors 0.08-0.22).
   An unmeasured lever's acceptance is unconstrained, so any sound
   selector spends >= #levers measurements. The proxy-ranking baseline
   (14-32% regret, 0% top-1) is this lower bound violated.
2. *The factorization follows from measured invariances.* Acceptance
   depends on regime, not cell (CV 2.2-3.3%) -> one profile per lever
   spans the grid; cost is cell-dependent (CV 14.5%, p90 19%) -> per
   cell, singles only; K is closed-form in the throughput identity
   S = tau(K)/(K R + 1). This yields sample complexity
   O(#levers + #confirmations): necessity from (1), sufficiency
   measured (space x12, cost x1.4).
3. *Composition is handled by INEQUALITIES used in their sound
   directions*, each with a quantified violation rate: f_comp >=
   prod(f_i) (128/134) admits; f_comp <= min(f_i) x 1.03 (117/134 at
   2%) eliminates; S <= (K+1)/(K R+1) at ANY acceptance prunes on cost
   alone (0/8 optima lost, up to 75.8% of the space removed). Point
   predictions between the bounds only ORDER the shortlist.
4. *The decision is made by measurement, not by the model.* No rule
   ranks the argmax first reliably (P5: 75-100% top-1 miss, every
   rule), so the shortlist is confirmed and the pick is a measured
   quantity; ranking error only costs regret when the true best misses
   the list entirely — the measured 0.5-4% tail.

The contrast with KnapSpec is not "heuristic vs theory": their
knapsack is an exact solver over premises this record falsifies
(additive value P2, additive cost P3, proxy inputs phase 90). Ours
inverts that — approximate ordering over premises that are derived,
measured, or bounded, with the decision itself measurement-grade.

## evaluation

Oracle: exhaustive factorial over a reduced but COMPLETE space —
quant{none,q1,q2} x window{none,512,2048} x skip{none,b2} x K{2,4} =
36 boots per architecture, each covering the 9-cell batch x ctx grid.
Complete on all four architectures (dense-8B, Llama-3.1-8B, MLA
V2-Lite, MoE 30B-A3B), each REPLICATED on a second independent content
sample (doc offset 16) for winner's-curse-proof truth scoring. The
llama oracle is the POST-FIX re-measurement (its original run had 12
w8int8 arms corrupted by a torch-compile cache-key collision; see the
correction note below) — every llama number in this document derives
from it. Regret is measured against the true optimum within that
space; the deployable space is ~40x larger and is addressed by the
scaling claim, not by this oracle.

### headline values

**The candidate set is singles UNION compositions** -- the search picks
whichever wins, and at low-cost cells the best single is statistically
TIED with the best composition (llama b1/2k +1.1%, b8/2k +2.6% — and
three b1 margins dissolve into content noise below). C2 is not a claim
that one should always compose; it is a claim about finding the best
member of the joint space.

**Composition beats the best single lever where the cost model says
it should** (positive gain in 8/8 cells on both architectures):

| arch | cells >= +5% | peak gain |
|---|---|---|
| dense-8B | **6/8** | **+31.1%** (b8/14k: 1.24 -> 1.63) |
| Llama-3.1-8B | **6/8** | +20.2% (b8/8k: 1.08 -> 1.29) |

Gain is CELL-STRUCTURED: over the two complete oracles, mean +4.2%
at 2k ctx vs +14.2% at 8k and +16.9% at 14k; +7.4% at b1 vs +15.8%
at b8. (The Gate-1 probe read b1 and 2k as ~0; the exhaustive
factorials found triples the probe never nominated, and the residual
b1 means lean on single-document draws — three of the six b1 winner
margins dissolve into content noise on multi-draw re-measurement,
see the winner-map section.) Composition pays only where TWO cost
terms bind simultaneously (long ctx -> KV term, mid-batch -> weight
term); where one lever already removes the single binding term, the
second cuts nothing — llama b1/2k is a tie (+1.1%, and the term
decomposition below shows why: tau unchanged, R slightly worse). At
llama b32/2k composition also RESCUES a cell where the best single
loses to AR (0.98 -> 1.04).

**The search finds it cheaply** (per-cell acceptance form — see the
reconciliation section; the figure's pooled-profile curve reads
slightly higher):

| method | dense mean regret | llama mean regret |
|---|---|---|
| best-single-only (the C1 policy) | 10.84% | 8.28% |
| search, confirm top-1 | 4.05% | 3.55% |
| search, confirm top-5 | **0.46%** | **1.61%** |
| random @ equal budget | 6.4-19.5% | 6.2-16.6% |

**The efficiency claim, stated as what is actually demonstrated:**
> search cost is O(#levers + #confirmations), INDEPENDENT of
> #compositions.

5 single profiles + N confirmations, and that count does not grow with
the composition count. On the 18-config oracle that is 16-22 of 36
boots (44-61%) — the reduced space is too small to show the saving.
Extrapolated to the deployable space (~16 singles, ~1384
compositions): ~21 boots, **~1.5% of exhaustive**. Direct
demonstration on the full space is Step 3.

### acceptance-free feasibility prune (Stage A')

From S = tau/(K*R+1) and tau <= K+1 (perfect acceptance), the best
speedup ANY acceptance rate can buy is bounded by COST alone:

> **S_max = (K+1) / (K*R + 1)**

so a config is hopeless iff S_max <= target; at target=1 this reduces to
**R >= 1** -- a draft costing a full target step can never pay, whatever
its acceptance. This prunes before any acceptance profiling, and because
additive R over-estimates cost the bound is already conservative-biased
(`--gamma` relaxes R further).

Measured, with regret UNCHANGED at every setting (so the prune is free):

| arch | pruned at gamma=1.0 | at gamma=1.15 (default) | optimum lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 31.9% | 6.9% | 0/8 cells |
| llama | 36.5% | 2.4% | 0/8 cells |
| MLA | — | **75.8%** | 0/7 cells |

MLA is the striking case: three quarters of its space is eliminated on
cost alone, which is the same fact C1 reports as "MLA is OFF-dominant"
arriving through the search rather than the grid. The prune's value
therefore scales with how infeasible an architecture is, and it is
largest exactly where exhaustive search would waste the most.

|---|---|---|

**The decisive result is not which aggregation rule wins -- it is that
INPUTS decide, not aggregation.** Proxy-ranked selection is
catastrophic (14-32% regret, never once picking the true best), while
three different aggregation rules fed MEASURED single-lever data all
land within 2.9-4.7%. This is the phase-90 measurement theorem
reproduced at composition scale, and it sharpens the KnapSpec
comparison: their published method pairs an additive knapsack with
COSINE-PROXY inputs; the knapsack aggregation is fine (2.9-4.4% here),
it is the proxy inputs that fail (14-32%). Which aggregation wins is
ARCHITECTURE-DEPENDENT: ours beats the knapsack on dense (0.46 vs
1.77 at confirm-5) and loses to it on llama (1.61 vs 0.79) — the
four-architecture section below makes that the result.

**Phase 98 locates where measured inputs stop sufficing.** Running the
knapsack against count-matched controls on the layer-skip lever with
DIRECTLY MEASURED per-layer retention (36 leave-one-out boots, Qwen3-8B,
`research/98_selector_demo/results_d2b_knapsack.md`), the product of
single-layer retentions ranks all six arms **perfectly — Spearman +1.000
at skip counts 4 and 8** — and then **inverts at count 16**, where its
predicted-best set (product 0.585, far the highest) measures tau 1.244
against a frozen alternating set's 2.080. So measured singles are
sufficient for aggregation up to a lever depth and insufficient past it,
which is a sharper statement than phase 90's control (rho -0.09 on ranking
set growth) because the boundary is placed: between 8 and 16 of 36 layers.
Two supporting facts from the same probe — skipping layer 0 ALONE drops
tau 8.083 -> 1.244, and every count-16 arm collapses to tau 1.0-2.1
against 6.4-7.4 at count 8, so that depth is outside the lever's usable
range regardless of which layers are chosen.

### reconciliation: pooled vs per-cell acceptance (a design refinement)

Two implementations of the same rule disagreed -- dense confirm-5
gave 2.58% regret with a POOLED acceptance profile and 0.46% with the
cell's OWN measured acceptance (llama post-fix: 1.95% pooled vs 1.61%
per-cell). The difference is real and instructive: acceptance is
cell-independent ENOUGH to profile once (CV 2.2-3.3%), but that
residual variation is INFORMATIVE, not noise -- using the cell-local
value is worth ~2 points of regret on dense, ~0.3 on llama.

Crucially it costs nothing: Stage A already boots each single AT each
cell to measure R, and the same boot yields that cell's acceptance. So
the deployed rule is:
  * use the cell's own measured acceptance when the cell has been
    probed (free, better);
  * fall back to the pooled per-depth profile only for cells not yet
    probed (cold start / transfer).
Reported figures use the per-cell form: dense 0.46%, llama 1.61% at
confirm-5.

### no universal SEARCH heuristic (four architectures)

With the MLA and MoE oracles built (18 configs x K{2,4} over the 9-cell
grid, 7 cells feasible, uniform protocol, physical-sanity gate clean),
the same three rules can be scored on four architectures instead of two:

| arch | optima lever-counts | ours | knapspec | product_rank | best |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense | {2:4, 3:4} | **0.46%** | 1.77% | 1.21% | ours |
| llama | {2:4, 3:4} | 1.61% | **0.79%** | 1.70% | knapspec |
| MLA | {2:7} | 0.94% | 9.68% | **0.41%** | product_rank |
| MoE | {0:2, 2:4, 3:1} | 0.69% | 3.85% | **0.34%** | product_rank |

**Each of the three rules wins somewhere and none wins more than two.**
That is C1's thesis -- no lever is universal -- recurring one level up,
in the SELECTOR. It is the same claim the aggregation-vs-inputs result
above makes about proxy inputs, but now about aggregation itself: among
rules fed measured inputs, which one ranks best is architecture-dependent.

**Why the rules differ, and why that explanation is not actionable.**
`ours` uses a PRODUCT of single-lever acceptance fractions (shrinks with
lever count) over an additive cost that over-estimates (also with lever
count), so it penalises multi-lever configs twice. `knapspec` sums both
value and savings, so it favours them. The bias that pays is the one
matching whether that architecture's optima are pairs or triples -- which
holds on 3 of 4 (it fails on llama). But the PREDICTOR is the lever-count
skew read off the single-sample argmax, and that argmax moved in 14/16
cells between content samples: dense's skew reads {2:5,3:3} or {2:4,3:4}
and llama's {2:2,3:6} or {2:4,3:4} purely by which truth is used. A
selector cannot be built on a statistic that unstable, which is why the
direct test of it failed (correlation -0.34 dense / +0.00 llama). So this
is a post-hoc EXPLANATION of the spread, not a usable rule.

**The dual-ranker union is RETRACTED.** It was adopted as a minimax
choice on two architectures (best worst-case 1.67% vs 2.50%/2.01%). At
four architectures it does not survive:

| rule | worst case across the 4 arches |
|---|---|
| **ours** | **1.61%** |
| union2 (ours + knapspec) | 1.64% |
| union3 (+ product_rank) | 1.64% |
| product_rank | 1.70% |
| knapspec | 9.68% |

Neither union beats plain `ours` on worst case. union3 is cheaper
(3.6-4.3 vs 4.6-5.4 confirms/cell) and better on MoE (0.89 vs 1.17) but
worse on llama (1.08 vs 0.32). `search.py`'s default is therefore
returned to `--ranker ours`; the union remains selectable but is not
recommended on present evidence.

**RESOLVED against 2-sample truths (second content samples measured).**
The table above is single-sample and carries winner's-curse inflation
(measured at 3.9-6.3pp); it is retained as the record. With the MLA and
MoE replications (36 boots each, doc offset 16, isolated cache roots),
every architecture now has a 2-sample truth — per-(config,K) mean S
over both samples — and each rule is scored twice, once picking from
each sample (`c2_truth_4arch.json`, figure `c2_ranker_4arch.pdf`):

| arch | ours | knapspec | product_rank | best (2-sample) | best (1-sample) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 1.74% | 2.35% | **1.73%** | product, by 0.01pp = tie | ours |
| llama | 2.42% | **1.42%** | 2.14% | knapspec | knapspec |
| MLA | 4.13% | 6.56% | **3.65%** | product_rank | product_rank |
| MoE | 3.88% | **3.43%** | 4.13% | knapspec | product_rank |

Three findings, in order of importance:

1. **Ranker choice is second-order, bounded by content noise.** The
   rule separation per architecture (0.6 / 1.0 / 2.9 / 0.7 pp) is at or
   below the pick-direction spread (~1-4 pp: dense picks-from-R1 score
   0.6-1.8% but picks-from-R2 2.7-2.9%; the sample you search FROM
   moves regret more than which rule you use). "No universal ranker"
   SURVIVES — winners still split 2/1/1 — but the split is itself
   within noise except one case: knapspec on MLA is genuinely dominated
   (6.6%, both directions). The winner identities also churned from the
   single-sample table (ours' dense win became a 0.01pp tie), which is
   the caveat's prediction confirmed.
2. **The search matches EXHAUSTIVE at half the cost, now on four
   architectures.** Committing to a single content sample's exhaustive
   argmax costs 0.55-5.67% on the 2-sample truth — on MoE (5.67% from
   R1) it is WORSE than every rule-based confirm-5 search run on the
   same sample (4.2-4.9%): the global argmax is the most
   winner's-curse-inflated pick in the space, while a shortlist confirm
   lands on less noise-lucky configs. The limit is content noise shared
   by search and exhaustive alike, not search error.
3. **MoE OFF-dominance replicates on independent content**: best
   composed S < 1 in 6/7 cells on R2 (5/7 on R1); the only surviving
   win is b32/2k (1.020 / 1.016) — C1's surface, twice.

The deployed default stays `--ranker ours` (the physical model, and
never dominated), documented as a second-order choice: any
measured-input rule plus confirm-5 lands within 1.4-4.1% of the
2-sample truth; only proxy inputs (14-32%) and knapspec-on-MLA are
excluded. This also moots the union question retracted above — there
is no reliable per-arch winner for a union to hedge between.

### ours-v2: replacing the fitted constants with the model (PRE-REGISTERED)

The 37b2d4df4 diagnosis located ours' one measured bias: additive cost
OVER-estimates composed R, the error grows with lever count, so the
winning triples get under-ranked (the acceptance side is accurate
within 1%). V2 was fully specified BEFORE scoring
(`research/94_composition/README.md`, 2026-08-05) and evaluated once,
on the truth_4arch protocol; implementation `scripts/ours_v2.py`,
results `paper/data/c2_ours_v2.json`. Both variants drop kappa
(measured neutral), shortlist by the OPTIMISTIC sound bound
min(f_parts) x 1.03 (so high-uncertainty triples must enter the
confirm list), and add two b1 content-robustness rules; they differ
only in the cost model:

- **v2r**: R for compositions PREDICTED by the roofline
  (theta/L_eff/sigma compose physically; 3 params fit from the searched
  sample's own singles — no oracle access);
- **v2g**: additive R divided by the structural gamma {2:1.15, 3:1.33},
  updated ONLINE from the confirmations the search already pays for.

2-sample mean regret (best baseline per arch in parentheses):

| arch | ours-v1 | best baseline | **v2r** | **v2g** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 1.74% | 1.73% (product) | 1.84% | **1.38%** |
| llama | 2.42% | 1.42% (knapspec) | 2.26% | 1.64% |
| MLA | 4.13% | 3.65% (product) | **3.47%** | **2.38%** |
| MoE | 3.88% | 3.43% (knapspec) | **3.41%** | 4.26% |

**Registered outcome: v2r PASSES all three criteria** — within 1pp of
the best rule on every architecture, strictly best on two (MLA, MoE),
never worst in both pick directions. v2g is sharper where it works
(best on dense and MLA — MLA 4.13 -> 2.38 — and within 0.22pp of
knapspec's llama win) but FAILS the no-failure-mode criterion on MoE
(4.26%, worst from-R2, tied-worst from-R1): the dense/llama-derived
gamma prior does not transfer to MoE's cost interactions, and five
confirmations per cell are too few to unlearn it. Reported as
registered.

Honest reading: fixing the measured cost bias moves ours from
worst-on-llama (2.42) to second (1.64) and halves its MLA regret — the
diagnosis was right — but no variant beats the content-noise floor
that bounds every rule. What v2 delivers is the registered goal:
**uniformly competitive with no decisively-dominated regime**. One
baseline shares that property — product_rank (within 1pp everywhere) —
and the honest comparison with it is: v2r wins both OFF-dominant
architectures (3.47 vs 3.65 MLA, 3.41 vs 4.13 MoE), ties within noise
on the GQA pair, and unlike product_rank its shortlist carries the
sound admission/elimination bounds and the content-robustness rules.
knapspec (MLA 6.6%) and proxy (everywhere) do have failure modes. The
deployable default becomes v2r: every constant in it is either
measured on the deployment's own singles or physically derived.

v2r is also integrated into the search harness (`search.py --ranker
v2r`; single-sample pooled curves): at confirm-5 it wins MoE
outright (1.01% vs 2.33%), edges MLA (0.84% vs 0.92%), and ties the
GQA pair (dense 2.69 vs 2.58, llama 2.10 vs 1.95). Two properties to
know: its confirm-1 regret is HIGH by design (6-13% — UCB shortlisting
is exploratory, so v2r needs confirm>=3), and the roofline prune
removes less than the additive one (28% vs 76% on MLA/MoE) because the
additive model's cost OVER-estimate was doing part of the old pruning
— the paper's prune numbers remain the conservative additive ones. The
CLI default stays `ours` (v1) so the committed curve artifacts and the
regret-vs-budget figure remain comparable across the record.

### mechanism: WHY composition wins or loses (term decomposition)

Decomposing S = tau / (K R + 1) for adding win512 to the quant single
(K=2; full table in `data/c2_decomposition.json`):

| cell | d(tau) acceptance | d(R) cost | d(S) result |
|---|---|---|---|
| llama b1/2k | **+0.0%** | **+0.5%** | -0.3% |
| dense b8/2k | -1.3% | -4.4% | +1.4% |
| dense b8/8k | -5.2% | -23.5% | +10.9% |
| llama b32/8k | -4.8% | **-48.4%** | +40.8% |
| dense b8/14k | -0.1% | **-49.8%** | +45.2% |
| dense b32/8k | -3.1% | **-57.6%** | **+55.3%** |

**Composition does not fail at b1/2k because acceptance drops -- tau is
literally unchanged (2.857 -> 2.857).** It fails because the second
lever has nothing left to cut: R moves +0.5%, i.e. very slightly
WORSE. At batch 1 x 2k the KV footprint is ~2k tokens, a negligible
share of the draft's cost, so windowing it saves nothing while the
scratchpad-gather machinery adds a small fixed overhead.

The general law, visible across every cell: **the acceptance cost of
composing is small and roughly constant (0-8%), while the cost benefit
ranges from nothing (+0.5%) to -58%.** Composition value is therefore
decided almost entirely by the DENOMINATOR -- by whether the added
lever's cost term is actually binding -- not by acceptance
degradation. This is the two-binding-terms law confirmed at the level
of the underlying terms rather than inferred from outcomes, and it is
what Stage-0 cell gating keys on.

### the b1/8k anomaly, RESOLVED: a single-document content draw

b1/8k appeared to break the pattern on BOTH architectures -- tau fell
21-24% there versus 3-8% at b1/14k. It is not a context-length effect
and not a property of composing. It is a sampling artifact, and the
resolution changes the verdict at that cell.

Three measurements settle it (data: `paper/data/c2_b1_8k_anomaly.json`,
figure: `paper/figures/content_variance.pdf`):

1. **A fine context sweep is jagged, not monotone.** Holding the stack
   fixed and sweeping ctx over 9 values, win512 dips at 4k and 8k but
   is fine at 6k, 7k, 9k and 12k. win8192 is flat (2.89-3.00) across
   all nine. Across-content CV: **win128 12.4%, win512 8.6%, win8192
   1.3%.** No length-monotone mechanism produces that shape.
2. **Re-measuring the SAME cell on different documents removes the
   dip.** At ctx=8000, identical stack and window, only the C4
   documents changed: tau goes 2.239 -> 2.891 -> 2.963 (+29%, +32%).
   The quant-only single measured on those same documents does not
   move (2.846 / 2.863 / 2.875).
3. **Batching averages it away.** b1 uses ONE document (the loader
   tiles `docs[0]` to the batch size); b8 uses 8 and b32 uses 16.
   dense win512 at 8k: 2.239 (b1) -> 2.805 (b8) -> 2.861 (b32); llama
   2.091 -> 2.740 -> 2.767.

**Mechanism.** Prompt packs are nested -- the 8k pack is a strict
prefix of the 14k pack -- so each context length truncates the same C4
stream at a different point and hands the draft a different tail. With
the full window the draft sees all of it and acceptance is
content-flat; with a 512-token window acceptance depends on whether
that particular tail is locally predictable. The 8k tail lands inside
a gemstone-glossary catalog whose format is established thousands of
tokens earlier. Both architectures dipped together because they share
the input, not because they share a mechanism.

**Consequence -- the exception dissolves.** Re-measuring the
composition itself at b1/c8000 on **9 document draws**, its acceptance
cost versus the quant single is **-4.6% (dense, CV 7.1%)** and
**-6.9% (llama, CV 7.8%)** -- against -21.7% / -22.6% read off the
original draw alone. That places b1/8k squarely inside the 0-8% band
the law above already claims holds everywhere, so it stops being an
exception rather than becoming a counter-example. The b1/2k result is
unaffected: it involves no acceptance change at all (tau 2.857 ->
2.857) and is a pure cost result.

**Exposure is bounded and now closed.** Sweeping every arm x batch in
the C1 grid for across-context spread, only win128 and win512 at b1
show it (CV 12.8-16.1%, collapsing to 1.8-4.6% at b8); every quant,
skip, kv-quant and wide-window arm sits at 0.3-5.7%. That is exactly
**4 exposed cells**, all re-measured on 3 document draws:

| cell | draw0 | +16 | +32 | mean | draw0 error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense/win128 | 2.120 | 2.353 | 2.607 | 2.360 | -10.2% |
| dense/win512 | 2.239 | 2.891 | 2.963 | 2.698 | -17.0% |
| llama/win128 | 2.064 | 2.789 | 2.741 | 2.531 | -18.5% |
| llama/win512 | 2.091 | 2.789 | 2.789 | 2.556 | -18.2% |

**Protocol rule adopted:** at b1 a cell is one document, so any
content-sensitive arm needs >=3 document draws there. Content-
insensitive arms do not -- their across-content CV is ~1-3%.

**This is also a result, not only hygiene.** Narrow-window drafts have
an order of magnitude more acceptance variance across documents than
quantized drafts (CV 12.4% vs ~1%) at equal mean. A deployment picking
levers on a single-stream workload can draw a bad document and lose
20% of its speedup, with no such risk from quantization. Variance, not
just mean acceptance, distinguishes the levers.

### what this costs the winner map (three b1 cells become ties)

The map selects per cell over singles + compositions. The **selection**
at b1/c8000 is unaffected -- its winner uses win2048, which is
content-insensitive, and correcting every win512 config upward by the
measured 20-22% draw error promotes none of them into the top six on
either architecture. What the content draw does threaten is the
reported **margin** at any b1 cell whose winner carries a 512 window.

Every b1 composition win was re-measured (winner AND best single) on
3 extra document draws. The llama rows were re-validated AFTER the
w8int8 repair, because the repair changed the llama map winners'
identity (`c2_map_validation_v2.json` supersedes the llama rows of
`c2_map_exposed_validation.json` / `c2_map_b1_8k_validation.json`;
dense is untouched — its oracle has no w8int8 arm):

| cell | single-draw gain | mean over 4 draws | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense b1/c8000 | +7.5% | **+5.2% ± 0.9** | holds |
| dense b1/c2000 | +7.1% | -1.7% ± 5.0 | within content noise |
| llama b1/c8000 | +8.5% | -0.2% ± 4.1 | within content noise |
| llama b1/c14000 | +7.2% | **+4.0% ± 1.4** | holds |
| llama b1/c2000 | +1.1% | +0.4% ± 2.7 | within content noise |

So at batch 1, **two of five composition wins survive multi-draw
measurement** — dense b1/8k and llama b1/14k, both won by
content-robust win2048 compositions — and three are ties. The llama
b1/8k row is the cautionary example: its single-draw margin was the
largest of any b1 cell (+8.5%) and it dissolves completely (-0.2 ±
4.1) — even a win2048+skip winner swings ±9% across documents at this
cell at K4. Ties are drawn hatched and labelled with the multi-draw
mean ± s.e.m. in `paper/figures/c2_winner_map_{dense,llama}.pdf`.

This does not touch the composition claim, which rests on the
mid-batch and long-context cells where the margins are +12% to +31%
and the winners are measured over 8-16 documents. It sharpens it: at
**batch 1 the composition advantage is real only where the winning
composition is content-robust AND the margin survives multi-draw
measurement**; a single-draw b1 margin of any size is untrustworthy.
The two-binding-terms law predicted b1 would be the weak regime for
compositions on cost grounds; content variance independently makes b1
the regime where a measured margin is least trustworthy.

### full-space demonstration (Step 3b)

The reduced oracle (18 configs) is too small to show the search's
saving -- 6 of its configs are singles the search must measure anyway.
Run the SAME search on the full dense space: quant{none,6} x
window{none,4} x skip{none,2} = 105 lever-combos x K{2,3,4,6} = **420
(config,K) boots exhaustive**.

| space | configs | exhaustive | search cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| reduced oracle | 18 | 36 boots | 16-22 (44-61%) |
| **full space** | **105 combos x 4 K** | **420 boots** | **27 confirmations (6.5%)** |

All 12 singles are REUSED from C1 Stage A -- same protocol, stack and
machine -- because acceptance is cell-independent and cost is measured
per single, not per composition. Charging the singles as if new still
gives 15.1%. **The space grew 12x; the search cost grew 1.4x.** That
is the O(#levers + #confirmations) claim, measured on both sides.

**Did the larger space pay?** Comparing the search's confirmed picks
against the reduced oracle's exhaustive optimum:

| regime | cells | mean gain over the reduced-oracle optimum |
|---|---|---|
| composition-LIVE (b>=8 & ctx>=8k) | 3 | **+4.1%** (peak +6.6%) |
| composition-DEAD (b1 or 2k ctx) | 5 | -1.7% |
| ungated, all cells | 8 | +0.5% |
| **with the Stage-0 cell gate** | 8 | **+1.5%** |

Honest reading: a fixed confirmation budget spread over a 12x larger
space is spread THINNER, so where the small space already held the
answer the wider search sometimes misses it (-1 to -3%). Where the
larger space genuinely contains better configurations it wins clearly
(+5.0%, +6.6%). This is exactly what Stage 0 (cell gating) exists for
-- gate on the measured two-binding-terms law and the wider search is
spent only where it pays, converting a +0.5% wash into +1.5% overall
and +4.1% on the live cells, at zero extra cost on the dead ones.

Caveat: 4 of the 27 confirmations (all Humming at K2 = verify width 3)
are blocked by the odd-width kernel wedge from the C1 ledger; the
affected cells use the search's next-best confirmed pick, so the
full-space numbers are a LOWER bound.

**The sampled audit (closes the extrapolation).** A fixed-seed uniform
sample of 40 of the 420 (config,K) points (seed 940805,
`c2_fullspace_audit_plan.json`; 37 booted, 3 reused, 0 missing after
retries) measures the search's distance from the FULL space directly
(`c2_fullspace_audit.json`): mean max-exceedance over cells **+0.48%**,
worst **+1.40%**. At 4 of 8 cells no sampled point beats the search's
pick — placing it in the top ~7% of the full space at 95% confidence —
and the cells where 1-2 points do beat it (by 0.0-1.4%, within content
noise) are the composition-DEAD 2k cells the Stage-0 gate deliberately
starves. The audit also confirms where the full space's value lives:
the best sampled configs are dominated by K3 — the depth the reduced
oracle never contained. Full-space regret is now a measured bound,
not a scaling claim.
